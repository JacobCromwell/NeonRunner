# D10 (the Beach skin): open questions

The owner asked for a Beach zone on October 9, 2026, with `docs/art/reference/beach_zone.jpg` as inspiration:
the gaps are pools of water, no new enemy assets, new background, floors, walls and ceilings, and its place
in the campaign still undecided. D10 is the skin only (`data/skins/beach_skin.tres`, shown with `--skin=beach`).
Everything below is a placeholder built to the GDD's rules, marked `## DESIGN-TBD:` in the code.

## Where the reference breaks the colour rule

GDD §5: only hazards glow in hazard colours (pink, yellow and black, red, orange, green, cyan), hazards keep
the same colour and shape language in every zone, and decorative things never read as a hazard warning. The
reference breaks that in five places; the skin follows the rule and departs from the reference.

- **Pool water doesn't glow.** The reference's pools glow turquoise. Cyan is the anti-grav pads' glow, and a
  glowing pool beside a pad would read as a pad. Placeholder: the water is deep unlit teal
  (`BeachSkin.water_color`, `PAT_BEACH_WATER`) with unlit glints, and everything inside a pool is darker than
  the darkest floor by a real margin (at most 40% of its luminance; `test_beach_skin` computes the budget from the
  skin's colours), so a pool reads as a hole like every gap. The turquoise survives where it is only scenery: the sea on the horizon (the sky).
  Should the water carry some glow after all (a faint violet or blue rim, say), at the cost of reading less
  like a gap?
- **Decorative neon is violet, blue or warm white only.** The reference's neon is pink, yellow, cyan, green
  and orange: all hazard hues. Placeholder: `neon_violet`, `neon_blue` and `neon_white` for the signs'
  glyphs and frames (`PAT_BEACH_NEON`), the only things in the zone that glow besides hazards, lamps and the
  cult's emblem and feed.
- **String lights and lanterns.** The reference's string lights are red, yellow, green and blue, and its paper
  lanterns glow in colours. Placeholder: string lights glow warm white, blue or violet; paper lanterns are
  unlit shells in muted colours (`lantern_shell_colors`) with a warm-white glow inside. Bunting and flags are
  unlit and muted.
- **Pool frames are flush.** The reference's tanks stand proud of the sand, in black steel frames. A raised
  rim would read as an obstacle that isn't there (GDD §3: safe things look safe, and damage happens only on
  hitbox contact). Placeholder: the tank is sunk flush; its coping is a dark steel line beside the orange
  edge (the same orange lip, strip and halo as every zone's gap edge, on the collision edge) so the edge
  pops against the bright sand. Should a pool be allowed a low frame that stands a little proud?
- **No words on signs.** The reference's signs say NEON TIKI BAR, FLAMINGO LOUNGE, ALOHA GEAR and the like.
  Placeholder: wordless silhouettes only (a sun, waves, a palm, a surfboard, a flamingo, a cocktail glass, a
  tiki totem), as the wordless broadcast of the cult's feed; the GDD has no lettering anywhere. Does the owner
  want legible signs (needs a font or glyph set, and a policy for what they may say)?

## Other design gaps

- **Time of day.** The GDD gives the Beach none. Placeholder: a bright tropical afternoon (`night_sky.gdshader`
  through its uniforms: a blue zenith, white cumulus, a warm sun glow toward the far end of the street, a
  turquoise sea and a low palm island on the horizon), with the lit walls, thatch and timber lifted by
  `bc_daylight` because the kit lights upright faces for a night city. Daylight is a risk for the game's
  look (every other zone is a night): the sky stays under the glow threshold and the sand mid-bright, so neon
  and hazards still bloom and stay the brightest things. Does the owner want it at dusk or night instead?
  `LevelConfig.sky` can give a level of the Beach its own sky, and `darkness` its own lighting.
- **The enemies' look (`enemy_variant`).** No new enemy assets, so the skin reuses an existing look:
  `&"city"` (the base Static TV Head cyborg, clean drones and trucks). Alternatives are `&"golden"` (the
  ceremonial enforcer) or a look of its own once the owner wants one (`CyborgSuit.look_for()`).
- **Pool depth: the water is reached.** The owner asked for "pools of water", and water six metres down hid
  behind the near edge from the game camera (4.2 m up) at any distance beyond a few metres: every pool read as a
  black pit. Placeholder: `pool_depth` 0.65 m (range 0.4-1.2), the water filled nearly to the rim like the
  reference's near-full tanks, so it shows from ten or more metres; above it a band of black rust-streaked tank
  steel (the reference's inner rim). A fall that ends a run (`fall_death_depth` 4 m) now goes on into the
  water: the runner (an Octodog, an Enforcer wreck) sinks out of sight into the opaque water plane, which reads
  as falling into the pool. The chase camera stays above it: `RunCamera` puts it at 4.2 m + 0.45 of the
  runner's height, 2.4 m above the floor even at the 4 m fall death (`test_beach_skin` pins it). The water is
  an unlit deep teal with slow ripples and soft glints, at most 40% of the luminance of the darkest sand or
  boardwalk (a real margin), so a pool is still a hole at a glance. Should a fall splash, or the water take
  more of the screen (a shallower pool still: 0.4 m)?
- **Ceilings.** Three kinds, with relative weights (`footbridge_weight` 3, `veranda_weight` 3,
  `barge_weight` 2): a boardwalk footbridge (across every lane), a veranda deck (narrow, reaching one wall) and
  a hovering party barge (any width, or reaching neither wall). The kinds and the barge (a cyberpunk tiki
  boat with engines in a pale violet-blue, far from the pads' cyan) are proposals; the reference shows only
  decks and verandas.
- **Doodads.** A surfboard rack (small), a beach cabana or a palm in a planter by `look_seed` (medium) and a
  tiki bar kiosk (large) are proposals for the GDD's three doodad classes; none has a face (so none can read
  as a cyborg or a citizen).
- **Boardwalk against sand.** The floor is sand with boardwalk runs over some lanes (`boardwalk_share` 0.2,
  runs of one to three `boardwalk_slot` (12 m) slots). The reference shows a sandy street with a boardwalk
  deck only along the buildings. The share is a guess; flat in every case.
- **The wall-run marks** (GDD §3: how high a wall run is) are 3.6 cm lines at 2 m and 4 m in a slightly
  darker shade of the wall (`wall_mark_color`, a multiplier of about 0.7): the kind you find when you look
  for it, like the Marketplace's, not a dark line. Is that readable enough in play?
- **More colour at street level, within the rules.** The street was one honey ochre. The walls now have seven
  bamboo tones and, in the band, painted doors and shutters in muted turquoise, coral and mustard (never a glassy blue, which would read as a window), surfboards
  painted flush on the wall (unlit coral, sea blue, turquoise, sage, mustard and cream), painted vertical boards
  and murals, planks and slats; above 8 m, clusters of paper lanterns (unlit shells, warm-white glow inside) and
  unlit striped awnings (a coral, turquoise or blue strip and cream) across the verandas' openings. The reference
  has far more saturated paint and neon; these stay muted (chroma at most 0.5, nothing near a hazard's colour)
  and flush or recessed in the band. The awnings are valances across the opening, not the reference's
  sloping ones that reach out over the street (nothing hangs out more than 0.25 m below 12 m).
- **The wall-run band's look.** The GDD wants the band calm: below 7.2 m (`band_top`) the shacks' faces are
  flush with marks at 2 m and 4 m (bamboo, mats, planks, rusty sheets, shut shutters, wordless posters, painted
  boards, murals), and the decoration (verandas, thatch, tiki masks, lanterns, signs, tanks) starts at
  `decor_min_height` (8 m). The reference's wall decoration is all at street level; at the runner's height
  it would break the band.
- **The hazard sign and fence.** The sign is the usual yellow/black frame around a painted surf or bar sign
  (wordless); the fence is the usual pink field between bamboo-wrapped steel posts in sand-filled drums. Both
  are the Beach's reading of the cross-zone hazard language, not new shapes.
- **The cult.** The emblem hides on some neon signs, roof billboards and the barge's hull (never smaller than
  `emblem_min_size`, 0.9 m, never a hazard colour); its feed plays on TVs behind some upper-deck bars and on
  roof billboards, never in the wall-run band. The shares (`emblem_share` 0.4, `feed_tv_share` 0.35,
  `feed_board_share` 0.3) are guesses.
- **Music.** None: D10 adds no zone, so no placeholder loop (CLAUDE.md: no new songs).

## Built beyond the brief (shared files)

- `night_sky.gdshader` gained three default-off uniforms: `skyline_hills` and `skyline_scale` (a skyline of
  smooth low hills instead of buildings: the Beach's island) and `abyss_depth` (how far below the horizon the
  sky becomes the abyss colour, 0.3 as before). No other zone sets them.
- `mesh_kit.gd` gained the Beach's pattern ids (80-89) and parameter encoders; `kit_solid.gdshader` includes
  `kit_beach.gdshaderinc` and dispatches on 80-89. A shared lesson for later skins: a pattern parameter that
  `floor()`s an exact multiple gets noisy on software rasterizers, and one above about 2^20 is imprecise
  (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, Zone skins, Pattern ids).
