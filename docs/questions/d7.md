# D7 — cult emblem and colour, owner's pick

**The question** (GDD §5, "The cult"): "Symbol and colour: *(open)*. An art agent will draw up a
few options for the owner to choose from." This file is that: four original options, drawn in code,
with a comparison sheet rendered for review. Nothing here invents story or lore beyond GDD §1 and
§5 — only the mark itself.

**The placeholder:** `data/world/cult_emblem_choice.tres` (`CultEmblemChoice.option`, marked
`DESIGN-TBD` in `scripts/world/meshes/cult_emblem_choice.gd`) is set to **A, "Broadcast Halo"**,
my recommendation below. It's a one-value change: set `option` to 1/2/3 for B/C/D and every skin
that calls `CultEmblem` with `choice.option` picks up the new mark and its default colours
automatically. Putting the chosen mark into the zone skins (hidden in logos and ads, shown openly
in the Golden Zone) is a follow-up task, not part of this one.

**Comparison sheet:** `tools/showcase/cult_emblem_sheet.tscn` (`godot4 --path . -s ...` or the
render commands in `CLAUDE.md`). Rendered to `build/cult_emblem_sheet/` (git-ignored; the
orchestrator has the images). Every option is drawn as code geometry in `CultEmblem`
(`scripts/world/meshes/cult_emblem.gd`), deterministic, no external assets.

**A note on originality:** two options went through a redesign after a first render showed a
real-world resemblance I hadn't caught on paper — see each option's note below. That's exactly why
this task renders a sheet instead of just describing shapes in prose: some collisions only show up
once you see the silhouette.

---

## The options

### A — Broadcast Halo (recommended)
A small core with three broken, concentric rings, each ring's gap rotated apart from the others.
**The idea:** it echoes the Resonator's own halos and its three-note chime (GDD §9.10), so the
cult's hidden mark and its broadcast altar are the same idea at two scales — the sermon and the
symbol are the same signal. **Hidden in plain sight:** broken concentric rings around a dot read as
a generic signal/network/broadcast icon, the single most common shape family in real telecom and
app logos, so it disappears into any wordmark without a second look. **Glowing** (neon ads): a
cool, barely-tinted white (low saturation, reads as sterile "premium tech" light, close to the
Corporate zone's own "cold and sterile white light," GDD §5). **Non-glowing:** brushed
platinum/chrome. **Golden Zone:** the rings become a gold sunburst medallion in relief, with a
small inset red stone at the core — reads as a radiant emblem on an archway or floor without any
new geometry family, just this shape at gold scale.

### B — Convergent Triad
Three notched, fletched arrows (a sharp tip near the centre, a concave notch at the feathered outer
end) in 3-fold symmetry, converging on a shared point. **The idea:** every path leads to him,
without saying so. **Hidden in plain sight:** converging arrows are a generic corporate "sync,
growth, alignment" mark — the kind of shape logistics and finance brands already use, so three
arrows pointing together never looks out of place. **Note on originality:** my first pass used
plain wide-to-narrow blades, which turned out to read as the Mitsubishi three-diamonds mark once
rendered; the version here — sharp inward tip, notched outward end — reads as arrows, not diamonds,
and its sharp points and open notch (rather than a broad blade curving off a central hub) also keep
it clear of the ionizing-radiation trefoil, which I checked deliberately given the 3-fold radiating
shape. **Glowing:** a warm filament white (a warm ~3000K tone, not a saturated yellow, so it reads
as warm tungsten signage, never a sign-hazard yellow). **Non-glowing:** brushed bronze. **Golden
Zone:** the arrows become polished gold, meeting at a small red centre stone like a pinned medallion
— reads well as a small pin or a large banner motif alike.

### C — Aperture Mark
Seven overlapping blade shapes around a small lens-ring opening, like a camera aperture or iris —
deliberately not an eye. **The idea:** the cult, and the villain behind it, are always watching and
always broadcasting; an aperture carries both without drawing either literally. **Hidden in plain
sight:** aperture/lens marks are everywhere in photography, optics and broadcast-tech branding, so
this reads as a generic "vision/focus/broadcast" logo. **Note on originality:** I first drew this
with 6 blades; six-fold radial symmetry sits close enough to a hexagram (Star of David) that I
moved it to 7, which also happens to be a common real lens-aperture blade count, and sits clear of
both the hexagram's 6-fold and the pentagram's 5-fold symmetry. **Glowing:** a deep sapphire-indigo
(noticeably darker and more saturated than the UI's pale azure, and further still from the anti-grav
pads' cyan). **Non-glowing:** dark gunmetal with a lighter chrome rim. **Golden Zone:** gold
aperture blades around a garnet-red lens — reads as a jewelled brooch or a ceiling medallion.

### D — Signal Spire
A slim vertical spire with a beacon tip, beside three short bars of increasing length climbing just
one side. **The idea:** echoes the Resonator's own silhouette (a spire) plus a "signal bars" glyph
— broadcast strength, not a religious object. **Hidden in plain sight:** the most throwaway-looking
of the four, closest to a plain telecom "antenna/signal strength" icon, so it's the easiest to bury
in a tiny corner of a logo or a HUD-style ad without anyone registering it as the same mark twice.
**Note on originality:** my first pass used three bars crossing straight through the spire,
narrowing toward the top — which, rendered, reads unmistakably as a multi-barred (patriarchal /
Russian Orthodox) cross, especially at small size. The version here keeps the bars off the spire's
centreline entirely (they attach to one side only, never crossing it), which reads as a signal-
strength icon at any size and never a cross. **Glowing:** a muted violet-plum (well short of the
fence's hot, fully-saturated pink in both hue and saturation/value — see
`tests/suites/test_cult_emblem.gd`). **Non-glowing:** dark oxidised plum-grey metal. **Golden
Zone:** a tall gold pillar relief with a single red accent partway up — fits an archway keystone or
a banner finial.

## Recommendation

**A, Broadcast Halo.** Reasons, in order:
1. It is the sharpest expression of "insidious" in the brief: the cult's hidden mark and its
   broadcast altar (the Resonator) are literally the same shape at two scales, so a player who
   later spots the Resonator can look back and realise the "harmless" logos were never harmless.
2. Of the four, it is the most convincingly generic corporate mark on first glance (broken rings
   around a dot), so it clears "hidden in plain sight" most comfortably.
3. It translates best into the Golden Zone's architecture — concentric rings read naturally as a
   sunburst carved into an arch, a floor medallion or a banner, at any size, without needing extra
   framing elements the other options benefit from (B and D's off-centre compositions want a bit
   more surrounding space to read well small; see the sheet's "at small sizes" column).

B is my second choice — the "convergence" idea reads clearly and it is the boldest option at very
small sizes. Either is a safe pick; C and D are viable but slightly weaker on the "instantly reads
as a generic mark" test.

## Risks and follow-ups
- **Colour is chosen per option, not fixed independently of it.** If the owner picks a different
  option than A, `CultEmblemChoice.option` is the only thing to change; `CultEmblem.default_scheme()`
  carries that option's own neon/metal colours with it. If the owner instead likes one option's
  *shape* but a different option's *colour*, say so and I'll (or a follow-up task will) split them.
- **The Golden Zone's gold/red (`CultEmblem.GOLD_COLOR` / `GOLD_ACCENT_COLOR`) are shared by every
  option** on purpose, so the comparison sheet's gold-relief column compares shapes, not colours.
  They're placeholder tones (not yet matched to D6a's actual Golden Zone skin, which doesn't exist
  yet) and may want a small retune once D6a lands.
- Working the chosen mark into the actual zone skins (hidden in Neon City/Gangland/Marketplace/
  Corporate/Dead Zone logos and ads, shown openly in the Golden Zone) is explicitly out of scope for
  D7 and is its own follow-up task once the owner has picked.
