# D8: music for the four new zones, the death dip and the level-complete riff per zone

The placeholders the task built, for the owner to review. Nothing below changes gameplay. "From build
phase 2" item 15 (zones without a track fall back to the menu music) no longer comes up: every zone has
its own track now.

1. **Music style per zone** (GDD §5, §11; OPEN_QUESTIONS §6 and FB 54, which stays open for a later
   design round). All the tracks are placeholders in the crunchy 16-bit / heavy-metal style, one seamless
   loop per zone, levelled to the same loudness. The existing three are unchanged: the menus at 100 BPM
   in A minor (synthwave), the City at 160 BPM in E minor (galloping synth-metal) and Gangland at 120 BPM
   in D phrygian (drop-D industrial groove). The new four:
   - **Marketplace** (a bustling, happy market): **144 BPM, Bb major**, 40 s. Bouncy ska-metal: a Mega
     Drive horn section playing the theme in thirds over off-beat guitar chops, an organ bubble, a walking
     slap bass and a son clave; then a punk-polka chorus of ringing power chords under a soaring horn hook,
     a breakdown where the horns answer guitar stabs, and the theme on lead guitar.
   - **Corporate** (a harder Blade Runner, corporate and military): **112 BPM, B minor (drop-B)**, 42.9 s.
     Cold cyber-metal: a machine riff of palm-muted sixteenths accented in threes, locked to the kick,
     with a sequencer bass and a sterile pad; then a heavy half-time march (Bm G Em F#) with timpani,
     snare rolls and a Vangelis-style synth-brass theme; then a military snare cadence over marching
     stomps before the machine returns.
   - **Dead Zone** (eerie, quiet, haunting): **84 BPM, C# minor**, 45.7 s. Quiet but never empty: a
     heartbeat and a soft ticking sixteenth pulse under a drone, a smoky pad and a lonely clean guitar
     with tremolo and a long echo (distant girders creak, wind, embers); then a slow, crushing doom riff on
     the tritone with a cavernous snare and a wailing lead, whose last chord rings out into the quiet
     again. The quiet half sits about 5 dB under the doom half.
   - **Golden Zone** (decadent opulence; it also plays in the Golden Palace): **132 BPM, F# harmonic
     minor**, 43.6 s. Neoclassical metal: harpsichord arpeggios, a string section and timpani under a
     regal, dotted theme on strings and lead guitar (a baroque circle of fifths), guitar sweeps over
     double kick, a harpsichord interlude, and a run down the harmonic minor scale from the Neapolitan G.
     It has no bells or chimes, so it never sounds like the Resonator's three-note chime (§9.10).

   Placeholder: `tools/asset_gen/track_<zone>.gd` (each header says the same), rendered to
   `assets/music/<zone>.wav`; levels and tempos in `data/audio/music_library.tres`; `DESIGN-TBD` in
   `tools/asset_gen/music_gen.gd`. Is each one's direction right for its zone?

2. **The death dip** (GDD §11: the music dips when the player dies; FB 53, with FB 52's mix balance
   tuned after playtesting). Placeholder: as the player dies, the zone's track sinks 10 dB over 0.5 s
   (under the death's dive-bomb sound) while a low-pass on the Music bus closes to 800 Hz, so it sounds
   far away rather than stopping. It holds under the revive offer, and comes back over 1 s on a revive or
   a retry (quick play's restart too). Leaving to the summary crossfades to the menu music at its full
   level, as before. With the pause menu's -8 dB duck, the deeper of the two applies, never the sum.
   The numbers are `dip_db`, `dip_lowpass_hz`, `dip_time` and `dip_recover_time` in
   `data/audio/music_library.tres` (F6, "Music"), marked `DESIGN-TBD` in `scripts/audio/music_library.gd`.
   Is that depth and muffling right, and should the dip start at the death (as built) or only when the
   revive offer appears a second later?

3. **The level-complete riff in each zone's key** (GDD §11; FB 53). Placeholder: the same riff everywhere
   (two palm-muted chugs on the root, one on the third, then the fourth rings out), in each track's key,
   on its beat and with a touch of its sound:
   - City: the existing E riff (E E G A), also the fallback for any music without a riff of its own
   - Gangland: D D F G in heavy eighths at 120 BPM, crushed, with an anvil
   - Marketplace: Bb Bb D Eb in eighths at 144 BPM, with a horn-section stab and a tambourine. The third
     is the major third (D), to stay in Bb major; every other riff uses the minor third, like the E riff
   - Corporate: B B D E (drop-B) in sixteenths at 112 BPM, with a cold synth-brass swell and a timpani
   - Dead Zone: C# C# E F# in sixteenths at 84 BPM, darker, dying away over a low boom and a tremolo
     guitar, with no crash
   - Golden Zone: F# F# A B in eighths at 132 BPM, with a harpsichord flourish and a timpani

   A level's end and a boss's defeat play the riff of the music that is playing. The riffs sit over the
   music at the E riff's loudness, without ducking it, as before. Placeholder: `level_complete_<track>` in
   `tools/asset_gen/sfx_bank_riffs.gd` and `data/audio/sfx_library.tres`. Are the riffs recognisably the
   same, and should the Marketplace's keep its major third?

4. **The Dead Zone's quiet half and The Hush** (GDD §5: Dead Zone 2 has "long silent stretches broken by
   sudden threats"; task R5). Placeholder: both Dead Zone levels play the same track. Levelled like the
   others, its quiet half sits about 4 dB under the other tracks and its doom half about 1 dB over. For
   The Hush's silent stretches, should its level play only the quiet half, a quieter mix, or the music
   dipped during those stretches (the director could dip on the level's signal the way it dips on a
   death)?
