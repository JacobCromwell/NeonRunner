# H8: Weapons hit hosts: open questions

Task H8 (GDD §9.7, owner, October 8, 2026): auto-fire targets hosts like any other cyborg, and a weapon kill
releases the Bad Dream. This supersedes FB 71 (hosts immune to all weapon damage) and item 71's note that
auto-fire never aims at hosts.

- **The score for a weapon kill of a host** (GDD §9.7: "Killing a host with a stomp, claws or the dash still
  earns a big score bonus"; it doesn't say what a weapon kill earns). Placeholder: a weapon kill (a direct hit
  or a heavy missile's splash) pays an ordinary cyborg kill's score (200) and no host bonus
  (`CyborgTuning.weapon_host_bonus` = 0 in `data/enemies/cyborg.tres`, in the F6 panel, `DESIGN-TBD` in
  `scripts/enemies/cyborg_tuning.gd`; `Cyborg.host_bonus_for`). A stomp, the claws or the dash still pay
  1,500 on top of the kill. Should a weapon kill earn a smaller host bonus, or nothing?

- **Should an Octodog's lunge or a Buzz Overdrive's charge kill a host?** (GDD §9.7; the owner's October 3
  revision made charges hurt other enemies). A charge is no weapon, so the placeholder keeps hosts out of a
  charge's reach, as before (`Enemy.charge_can_hurt`, `DESIGN-TBD` in `scripts/enemies/enemy.gd`): G7's
  planted cyborgs are never hosts, and hosts still never board an Enforcer Truck. If a charge should kill a
  host, it would release a Bad Dream nobody chose to release (no player kill, no bonus): is that wanted?

- **Where a chase begins after a weapon kill** (GDD §9.7: the Bad Dream "bursts out of a host cyborg when the
  host is killed"). Weapons kill hosts well before the runner reaches them: measured over the campaign's host
  levels (Dead Zone 1–2, Golden 1–3) at 3, 5 and 6 lanes, weapon tier 2 kills each host 1.4–1.5 s of run
  before its spot (about 37 m), tier 3 1.0–1.1 s, tier 4 1.1–1.3 s (`tools/measure/host_releases.gd`). The
  generator plans each chase, its anti-grav pads and what it keeps off chases from the host's spot
  (`host_rules.gd`). Released there, a chase began up to 37 m early: in 12 of 45 weapon runs inside a wall
  fence's drop window the generator keeps off chases, with up to 10.2 s without a pad (the guarantee is 10 s),
  and the Bad Dream flew back from far ahead to its first slash. Placeholder (`DESIGN-TBD` in
  `scripts/enemies/bad_dream.gd`): a Bad Dream that bursts out further ahead than its hover spot (7.5 m)
  rises out of its host as usual, then **lurks** over that spot, harmless and facing the runner, holding no
  other attack back, until the runner comes within 7.5 m; only then does its chase begin (its 20–30 s clock,
  its emergence time and first slash's delay, its big attack), exactly as for a stomped host. Measured: every
  chase then begins at most 14 m before its host's spot (a stomp: up to 9 m, as the host walks toward the
  runner), with the same pads as a stomp's, nothing kept off chases met, no chase fizzled or overlapping
  another, and no slash during an Octodog's charge or a drone's barrage. The alternatives were planning
  every chase from the earliest possible release (the longest weapon range, about 78 m earlier: fewer hosts,
  doodads, cuts and wall fences around every host in all five levels, and the Bad Dream still slashing from
  far ahead), or holding auto-fire on a host until it's close (not "like any other cyborg", and the laser
  would rarely kill one). Is the lurk right, or should the chase start at the kill?

- **Laser tier 1 hardly ever kills a host.** A late-campaign host takes 7 tier-1 shots, and tier 1's 42 m
  range leaves about 2 s before the runner arrives: in the measured runs tier 1 never killed a host before
  the runner reached it (the runner stomped them all). So the cost of carrying the weapon into host levels is
  real from tier 2 on (the missiles reach 70 m). Is that as intended, or should tier 1 reach hosts too?

- **The first-encounter hint** (`data/hints/hints.json`, `host`). It read "Glitching cyborgs carry something
  worse. Think before you stomp one.", which no longer tells the whole story. Placeholder: "Glitching cyborgs
  carry something worse, and killing one sets it loose. Your weapon fires at them too: switch it off in the
  shop to leave them be." Is the wording right?
