# K1: the Bad Dream hits a runner on a ceiling

The owner's request (October 2026): the Bad Dream (the Cyborg's apparition) must be able to hit the player while they
ride a ceiling. GDD §9.7 used to say "Cannot reach the ship's hull. It waits below until the player drops" (and §10's
Sleep Taker note "It can't reach the ceiling (the Bad Dream rule)", already replaced by the no-ceilings arena). The
owner's decision overrides that rule; the orchestrator edits the GDD.

What was built (`BadDream`, `BadDreamTuning`, `data/enemies/bad_dream.tres`; the generator, the pads and the Sleep Taker are
untouched): a runner on a ceiling gets the same telegraph as one on the floor (the shriek, the maw, the covered lanes
lit red, here on the ceiling's underside, and the dream rearing up toward the hull with its arms raised), the same
three lanes (the rider's and one either side, clamped at the street's edges) and the same lunge; the claws hang from the
underside. Only leaving the locked lanes dodges it (a jump away from the ceiling doesn't, as a floor jump doesn't). A
ceiling has no wall to run to and nothing drops a rider at will, so the "no escape left" rule is: a ceiling lane outside
the band, under the ceiling, with nothing solid in it.

1. **A three-lane street's middle lane, and narrow ceilings.** The three-lane slash covers every ceiling lane for a rider
   in the middle of a 3-lane street (the phone), and for any rider on a ceiling three lanes wide or narrower whose band
   takes in the whole ceiling (a 5-lane street's ceiling over lanes 1-3, rider in lane 2). Placeholder (DESIGN-TBD,
   `BadDream._escape_open`): no slash comes then (the existing "no escape left" rule, which on the floor is saved by
   the walls), so such a rider can't be hit on that ceiling. Should the slash instead narrow to two lanes there (the
   rider's and one side, leaving a lane free), or does a rider in the middle of a phone ceiling stay safe? On the
   phone, where a pad's ceiling covers all 3 lanes, the placeholder makes the middle lane the only refuge.
2. **How the slash is timed against the pad.** Placeholder (DESIGN-TBD, `ceiling_settle_time` 0.5 s): it doesn't
   telegraph until the runner has been up that long since their pad flipped them (the flight up takes about 0.4 s), so
   a warning isn't spent in the air; and it doesn't telegraph unless the ceiling lasts over their lane until the claws
   have passed (a rider falling off the end would fall through them), nor with a return pad about to drop them. With the
   host rules' 3 s ceilings (`pad_ceiling_seconds`) a slash is possible only from 0.5 s to about 1.3 s after the
   flip (estimated from the numbers, not measured on generated levels), and only when its 3-4 s timer is due then: a
   host-chase ceiling is a short stay the slash only sometimes reaches. Is
   that right, or should the generator's chase ceilings be longer now that the ceiling is no refuge (a generator change,
   not made here)?
3. **The anti-grav pads' role.** GDD §9.7 guarantees pads "during the chase" and §3 calls the ceiling the escape from
   it; the pads are unchanged (the generator, `HostRules`), but a ceiling now dodges a floor slash that was telegraphed
   while the runner was on the floor (they're out of the locked floor lanes) and no more. Is a pad still the chase's
   guaranteed way out, or what is it for now?
4. **The look of its reach.** Placeholder (DESIGN-TBD, `hull_clearance` 0.6 m, `ceiling_rise_speed`
   3 m/s, `ceiling_slash_reach` 1.75 m): it rears up until its head is 0.6 m under the underside (never into the hull),
   its arms stay up and sweep across instead of down, and its claw streaks hang from the underside. Between slashes,
   with a rider on a ceiling, it floats as usual (maw shut, head tipped back to look up) where it used to wait with its
   maw open "reaching up at the hull it can't reach". Is the rearing-up right, or should it rise further into the hull?
