1. **The space between the stash's two doodads.** How long should it be? (GDD §3, Ceiling scenarios: "a doodad, a
   space, then a second doodad, and then the treasure".)
   Placeholder: the most a dash that smashes the outer doodad still carries the runner past its end, so no single
   dash smashes both (`TreasureStashRules.dash_space`): the dash's 0.6 s at the run speed plus its 8 m/s (the same at
   every tier), the most a speed pad's or a ramp's fading boost adds (2.9 m), a body's length (the latest dash that
   still smashes it steers into its side beside its end), a frame, and `outer_space_margin` (0.5 m): 23.1 m at the
   Casino's 23 m/s to 24.4 m at the Golden Zone's 25 m/s, about a second of run. A shorter space would let a dash
   started late enough smash both. The outer doodad is a medium one (`outer_size`), as the stash's own.

2. **The lanes beside the space.** Should the doodads alongside (in the two neighbouring lanes) also run beside the
   outer doodad and the space, so nobody slips into the space sideways? (GDD §3, the same.)
   Placeholder: they don't; the walls stand beside the second doodad and the treasure only, as before. A runner the
   outer doodad pushes, or one whose dash ended in the space, meets the second doodad's push with room beside it, as
   a runner in the stash's lane always did. So a floor runner still gets in the way the owner allowed ("a floor runner
   can still get in that way; it is just harder"): around the outer doodad in the next lane, into the space, then a
   dash through the second one (test_ceiling_scenarios rides it). Walled beside the space instead, a runner whose dash
   ended there would meet the second doodad's push with no room on either side (the push would carry the body into a
   wall doodad), unless the walls stopped short of it, leaving the opening right where it matters; and the only way in
   from the street would be a dashing switch through a wall doodad. Is the open space the "harder" the owner meant?

4. **Who the wall-hop credits are for.** The GDD says "high-value credits further along than one wall run reaches, so
   only a player inventive enough to wall hop collects them", but a side wall can be stepped onto anywhere, so a
   runner who just steps on further along would reach credits that are only further along. (GDD §7, §3 Wall hop.)
   Placeholder: they also hang higher than a fresh wall run carries a pickup without a hop, 3.11 m
   (`WallHopPlacement.gem_height`: a step on from the floor tops out at 2.2 m, straight back on as a run drops its runner
   at 2.44 m, a pickup reaches 0.575 m, and `gem_clearance` 0.1 m), and past one standard wall run's reach (2.16 s,
   no claws) from the line's entry. A hop starts a fresh run higher (the air entry: 60% of the height it hops from on
   top), so a hop from anywhere above about half a metre gets there; a single run with claws slides on further but
   passes under them. A jump onto the wall from the floor also starts a run high enough (up to 3.16 m), so that
   reaches them too (as would a ramp's launch or a hover truck's roof, which the line keeps away from). Should they
   hang higher still, above a jump's entry (about 3.8 m), which only a runner who hops early and often reaches, or is
   distance alone what the owner meant (then stepping back on further along takes them)?

9. **How soon a Resonator's first pulse still owed keeps its turn** (deferred item 350; GDD §9.10, §9 big attacks take
   turns). **Changed** (the orchestrator's decision, task N1): the wait after which a first pulse still owed keeps its
   place in the director's turn queue while it waits for clear floor (`Resonator._first_overdue`) is now its own
   tunable, `ResonatorTuning.first_pulse_wait_max` (`data/enemies/resonator.tres`, F6 "Enemy: Resonator"), at 0: it
   keeps its place as soon as it waits, so the other types' next big attacks wait for it and it pulses at the next
   clear floor. Before, the threshold was `turn_wait_max`, 8 s. `turn_wait_max` still drops a visit's later pulses
   and spaces the visits. **Why:** the stash's longer floor moved Golden 1 at 5 lanes on its own seed. Its third
   Resonator's first pulse, planned at 2919 m before, now comes past the stash, at 3069 m and then 3236 m, each in a
   stretch of clear floor under a second long. In test_resonator's campaign play a heli drone that has stayed since
   878 m took the turn in both before 8 s had passed. The floor was never clear again before the level's end, and the
   Resonator left without a pulse. On main it had pulsed only in the last of those stretches, which the drone happened
   to leave free. **Result:** it pulses there now (5 pulses from that build's 3 Resonators). Over Golden 1-3 at 3, 5
   and 6 lanes, on their own seed and 9101-9105 (54 builds, 97 Resonators, played as test_resonator plays them), one
   Resonator still never pulses, as on main: Golden 2 at 5 lanes on seed 9102. That one starves another way: a Bad
   Dream's chase the runner set off holds its first pulse, the floor then stays unclear for 22 s, and the next
   Resonator's arrival sends it away. That is item 350's other open point. Main has the same one; before this
   change the branch had two. test_enemy_director's no-overlap checks and test_resonator's (no two types' big
   attacks overlap in the Golden campaign plays) pass.
   Questions: is "as soon as it waits" right, given that the other types wait longer for a Resonator that owes its
