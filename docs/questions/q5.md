# Questions from task Q5 (the Ice Lab's Octodog jars that break open; the owner, October 21, 2026)

GDD §5, Zone 9, "Octodog jars" fixes what the owner asked for: to get extra value from the Octodog (§9.4), "some jars
hold a curled-up Octodog instead of a brain. Sometimes smashing an Octodog jar doodad releases its Octodog into the
world, and the runner has to fight it"; the level schedule lists Octodog jars on Frost Foundry's row, and §3 lists the
jars among the Ice Lab's doodads. GDD §5 also lists proposals the owner hasn't confirmed (a jar is smashed the way every
doodad is, by the dash; a jar that will release its Octodog looks the same as one that won't; the freed Octodog tumbles
out, runs ahead to its usual distance and starts its usual attack from the first wind-up, with its cue, never a lunge
without warning; it is fought and killed like any Octodog; jars on the walls are scenery only). They are built as
proposed. Each placeholder below is marked DESIGN-TBD where it lives and built so it's cheap to change; the dog's numbers
are F6 tunables (the Octodog's, "Jars"), the count a level setting. `tools/measure/octodog_jars.gd` measures every build
(`--seeds`, `--seed-base`, `--why`, `--without`).

1. **How many release, and where.** (GDD §5: "some jars", "sometimes".) Placeholder: `LevelConfig.octodog_jars` is 1 in
   each of the Ice Lab's three levels (`data/levels/ice_lab_*.tres`), with no start of its own (Frost Foundry has the
   first, its hint shown before it); only those jars hold an Octodog, and the skin's other Octodog jars (IceLabDoodads'
   variety, 0 to 9 a level, commoner toward the laboratory) hold nothing, so "sometimes" is the one among them. Why one:
   the heli drone's pad schedule (§9.6: "if the pad is missed, another appears 8–10 seconds later, repeating";
   drone_rules.gd keeps one every 8 to 10 s from its first pad to the level's end, and no Octodog's run may meet a pad or
   a landing, octodog_rules.gd) leaves no stretch long enough for a dog's two charges once it starts
   (about 8 s of run from the jar to the end of its second charge), so a freed dog's usual attack fits before the
   drone's first pad only, where the level's standing Octodog, the gantryway and teleporter introductions and the
   patterns already stand. Most jars stand in the first third of the level (fronts at 0.02 to 0.36 of it, a jar whose dog
   makes one charge anywhere). Quick play's prototype level takes the default 1, endless mode its level's 1 over its 20
   minutes, a weekly challenge's week Sentinel Row's (item 11). Should more of them release, a level start them later,
   or endless mode stand more?

2. **Which lanes.** (GDD §5 doesn't say.) Placeholder (`octodog_jar_rules.gd`): wherever a zone doodad may stand
   (`LevelGenerator.doodad_fits`): an inner lane, or an outermost one by an open stretch of side wall where the level
   stands doodads there (`doodad_wall_lane_share`), pushing inward. The freed dog comes out in the jar's lane. Should a
   jar that releases stand in the inner lanes only?

3. **What breaks a jar.** (Proposed: "smashed the way every doodad is, by the dash".) Placeholder: whatever breaks a
   doodad lets its dog out (`Octodog` watches the jar's entry for "smashed"): the dash, and a wall runner low on the side
   wall beside a jar in an outermost lane, who breaks it as they reach it (task L3). A runner who runs into it without
   the dash is pushed aside as by any doodad, and its dog leaves play unseen once they're past it. Should the wall
   runner's break let the dog out too, or should only the dash?

4. **The tumble.** (Proposed: "the freed Octodog tumbles out".) Placeholder (`data/enemies/octodog.tres`, "Jars"): it
   bursts out just past the jar in a spray of pale glass and green, flung `jar_tumble_speed` 7 m/s faster than the
   runner for `jar_tumble_time` 0.45 s in a `jar_tumble_height` 0.7 m arc, rolling head over heels once, harmless (no
   hitbox until it's ahead of the runner in its sprint), then runs on like a dog after a lunge.

5. **When its attack starts.** (Proposed: "runs ahead to its usual distance and starts its usual attack ... from the
   first wind-up".) Placeholder: its first wind-up is planned `release_distance` past the jar's front (61 m in the Ice
   Lab): the tumble, the sprint to its usual distance ahead (less what the tumble gained), `jar_settle_seconds` 0.5 s to
   line up, and the most the dash that broke the jar can carry the runner on. A dog in position sooner paces there until
   the planned moment, as any dog does.

6. **How many charges.** (GDD §9.4: "2–3 charges early, up to 4 at the maximum".) Placeholder: the freed dog tries for
   the count the level's enemy scaling rolls (3 to 4 in the Ice Lab) and makes as many as fit, 2 at least, as a standing
   dog must (octodog_rules.gd); where no spot in the level has room for 2 even with room made (item 7), it makes 1, so
   the level keeps its jar. Measured: 1 charge in 6 of 36 builds (the levels' own seeds and 9001-9003 at 3, 5 and 6
   lanes) and 11 of 36 (seeds 8801-8803); on the levels' own seeds, Frost Foundry and Specimen Vault at 6 lanes. Should a
   level with no room for 2 have no jar that releases instead, or should more give way (a standing Octodog's encounter,
   say, where the level has two)?

7. **Making room.** (Not in the design.) Placeholder (`octodog_jar_rules.gd`, `_best`): where nothing fits without it, the
   jar takes plain content out of its way, as a gantryway's introduction does: pattern cyborgs and window cyborgs and
   fence generators standing near one of its dog's charges or where the jar keeps clear (never a host, a gantryway's or a
   charge path's cyborg, an Octodog or a drone); ceilings the patterns put there, with their pads and the credits on them
   (never a drone's, a ceiling scenario's or one a Barnacle Turret hangs from); plain holes and fences in its charges'
   stretches and around the jar (never a wall-run gap's hole, a pulsing fence or one a fence generator powers). Never the
   last of a feature or the first of one the level introduces; the spot that takes the fewest out wins. Measured: 0 to
   18 pieces and enemies a level, 4 to 6 on average. Is that acceptable, or should a level without room simply have no
   jar that releases?

8. **One dog at a time.** (GDD §9: big attacks take turns; the director holds only other types'.) Placeholder
   (`Octodog._ask_turn`, `_another_dog_charging`): a dog's first wind-up waits while another Octodog's charges are on,
   its planned charges moving on as in any wait for its turn (`turn_wait_max`), else it gives up. This applies to every
   Octodog, standing ones too; the generator already plans dogs one at a time, so it changes nothing unless a wait moved
   one dog's charges into another's.

9. **The hint's wording.** Placeholder (`data/hints/hints.json`, "octodog_jar", shown before the level's first jar holding
   an Octodog, from Frost Foundry): "Some specimen jars hold a curled-up Octodog. Smash one with the dash ({dash}) and its
   Octodog may break out: it runs ahead and lunges like any Octodog, so watch for its wind-up, then sidestep ({move_left}
   / {move_right}) or jump ({jump})." It says "may", since nothing tells the jars apart.
