# Questions from R5 (the campaign's shape)

Unless said otherwise, numbers are averages over each level's own seed and 8 others at 3, 5 and 6 lanes (27 layouts
a level), measured with `tools/measure/level_shape.gd`. Counts that compare two settings are also given over 453
layouts a level (the own seed and 150 others), because a 27-layout average moves by about 0.8 enemies and 1.5
obstacle rows between two seed sets even when nothing changes.

**A level's newest things get the most picks** (GDD §5, owner's review P2 13)

1. **The recency curve.** Placeholder (`data/tuning/feature_recency.tres`, F6 "Feature picks (campaign)"): a
   pattern's pick weight is multiplied by 4 in the level that introduces its newest feature, 2.5 one level later,
   1.75 two levels later, 1.25 three, and 1 from four levels on. Is the shape right? Should the oldest features drop
   below 1 (they already lose some share to the newer ones)?
2. **No level gets easier: the curve moves picks within a kind.** Placeholder: the curve only moves picks between
   patterns of the same kind (`keep_share_by_kind`): patterns with enemies keep the number of enemies they place,
   those with obstacles only (holes, fences, signs) and the safe ones (a plain ceiling, a ramp, a speed pad) keep
   their share, and plain obstacles keep theirs. So a new enemy takes its extra picks from older enemies, a new
   mechanic from older mechanics. Over 453 layouts a level, no level has fewer enemies or obstacle rows than before
   R5 by more than 0.08 enemies or 0.04 rows (each within one standard error, 0.1–0.3 enemies and 0.1–0.5 rows;
   The Hush apart, whose remix has fewer enemies by design and where the curve changes nothing), and several have
   more: Gangland 1 +0.4 enemies and +0.7 rows, Gangland 2 +0.7 and +0.4, Gangland 3 +0.7 and +1.7, Marketplace 1
   and 2 +1.9 and +1.6 rows. The cost: a new safe mechanic can only take picks from other safe
   ones, so City 2's new ceilings (its only safe feature) get no more picks than before (they already had half of
   the feature picks). Is "within a kind" the right rule?
3. **Features the curve leaves alone.** Placeholder (`max_factor`, all at 1: never boosted): the host (one Bad Dream
   chase at a time), the hover truck (one at a time, one a level early on), the drone (waves apart) and the Octodog
   (one at a time, on a clear stretch), whose rules dropped most of the extra picks the curve gave them and left
   their stretch empty (Golden 1: 2.5 host picks a level instead of 0.8, all but about one dropped; −1.4 enemies and
   −3 obstacle rows), and the vent screech (rare, GDD §9.5). So the curve changes nothing in Dead Zone 1, The Hush,
   Corporate 2 or the Golden Zone until their own new enemies are built (their only new built feature there is
   capped), and Gangland 2's new Octodog keeps its old share (9% of the feature picks, fifth): the curve used to
   give it 14% and 0.9 more dogs a level, which its rules did keep there, but the level lost 0.9 obstacle rows.
   Should any of these get a boost after all (a cap above 1), or the Octodog only where it's introduced?
4. **The newest things' shares now.** The newest built feature is the most picked in City 2 (ceilings, 50%, as
   before), City 3 (pulsing fences, 30%; its window cyborgs second, 22%) and Gangland 1 (screeches, 33%; its ramps
   second, 20%), and second or third in Gangland 3 and Marketplace 1 (fence generators, 14%). Before the curve these
   were 29%, 17%, 27%, 16% and 8%. The curve multiplies each pattern's own weight, so a feature with many patterns
   can still out-pick a new one with a single light pattern (the screech's six patterns weigh 5.0 in all, the
   host's one 0.8). Should the curve set each feature's share directly instead?
5. **Older features lean on the guarantee a little more.** Built without the every-feature guarantee, Gangland 2 has
   no ceiling and no cyborg in 5 of 27 layouts (1 before), Gangland 3 no cyborg in 4 (2); other levels need the
   guarantee less than before (Marketplace 1 and 2: 13 and 18 fewer layouts missing something). The guarantee
   places every feature in every layout. Acceptable?
6. **Level weights on top of the curve.** Marketplace 2's vent screeches (2.5×) and Corporate 2's military (1.5×
   drones, hover trucks and Buzz Overdrives) still apply; the curve now leaves vent screeches, drones and hover
   trucks alone, so these weights are all that favours them. Keep both?

**Dead Zone 2, The Hush** (GDD §5: "fewer enemies but more hosts and Bad Dream chases, darker lighting, and long
silent stretches broken by sudden threats")

7. **Quiet stretches and bursts.** Placeholder (`data/levels/dead_zone_2.tres`): 14 s quiet stretches, patterns
   4 s apart, with no enemies but hosts, alternating with 7 s bursts of threats only (holes, fences, signs and
   enemies), 0.9 s apart, the spacing other levels reach only at full difficulty. Per 100 m: 0.03 enemies and 0.9
   rows of obstacles in the quiet stretches, 1.25 enemies and 2.1 rows in the bursts (the level used to average
   0.8 enemies and 1.6 rows everywhere). A level: 10.9 enemies besides its hosts (21.2 before) and 34 rows of
   obstacles (41). With fewer picks, more of its features come only from the every-feature guarantee: built without
   it, a layout has no fence generator in 17 of 27 (2 before), no speed pad in 14 (4), no cyborg in 7 (2), no
   window cyborg and no screech in 5 (1) and no Octodog in 24 (19); the guarantee still places each one. Right
   lengths and densities? Bursts could go tighter than 0.9 s, which no level has used yet (not playtested).
8. **Threats that outlast a burst.** A hover truck (20–30 s), a drone (until a pad hurls it) and a Bad Dream's
   chase (20–30 s) that start in a burst carry on into the next quiet stretch. An Octodog needs a clear floor for
   its charges, which the dense bursts rarely leave, so its rules find its spot in a quiet stretch about half the
   time (0.5 Octodogs a level there, 0.5 in bursts). Acceptable, or should a quiet stretch wait until they're gone?
9. **More Bad Dream chases.** A chase starts only when the player kills a host on purpose (GDD §9.7), so The Hush
   has more hosts: 1.45 a level over 453 layouts (Dead Zone 1: 1.09; The Hush before: 1.0), two or three in 42% of
   its layouts. Every one stands in a quiet stretch, where stomping, clawing or dashing into it is easy.
   Placeholder: hosts picked 12.5× as often (3.9 host picks a level); the extra picks that don't fit leave their
   quiet stretch emptier, never a burst. The host rules fit one chase at a time (31 s plus 7 s before the next
   host), and none across the first drone wave's arrival (the drones own the pads from then on, GDD §9.6), so
   three is the most in practice. More chases would need shorter chases, fewer or later drones in The Hush, or
   more reason to kill a host (a bigger bonus). Which, if any?
10. **Darker lighting.** Placeholder: The Hush's `darkness` 0.7: the scenery gets 51% of its light (the sky, the
    distance fog and the skin's lit surfaces; on screen the floor is about 23% darker, the sky about a third
    darker), never below 30%; glows, the runner and the enemies keep their light. Right amount? The Dead Zone's
    skin (task D5) sets the zone's own palette, and the level's darkness applies on top of it.
11. **Music in the silent stretches**: the zone music's task (D8) asks the same in open question 112 (only the Dead
    Zone track's quiet half in The Hush, a quieter mix, or the music dipped during its silent stretches). If the
    music should follow the stretches, the generator lists them (`LevelGenerator.quiet_stretches()`), so the run can
    tell the music player when each one starts and ends.

**The Octodog's wait for its turn** (R3's big attacks take turns, GDD §9)

12. **Moving on after a wait.** A dog held for another type's turn now keeps asking every frame until its turn
    comes, and if the wait made it miss its planned clear stretch, its charges keep moving on with the player until
    the stretch ahead is clear again, within the same `turn_wait_max` (4 s) as the wait itself; then `charge_slack`
    (40 m) runs as before. So a dog may pace ahead of the player for up to about 6 s before its first charge
    (before: it gave up without charging when a fence came into its moved-on window). Is that long wait fine, or
    should a dog that waited re-plan its charges further ahead instead?
