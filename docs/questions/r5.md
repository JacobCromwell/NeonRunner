# Questions from R5 (the campaign's shape)

Numbers below are averages over each level's own seed and 8 others at 3, 5 and 6 lanes (27 layouts a level),
measured with `tools/measure/level_shape.gd`.

**A level's newest things get the most picks** (GDD §5, owner's review P2 13)

1. **The recency curve.** Placeholder (`data/tuning/feature_recency.tres`, F6 "Feature picks (campaign)"): a
   pattern's pick weight is multiplied by 4 in the level that introduces its newest feature, 2.5 one level later,
   1.75 two levels later, 1.25 three, and 1 from four levels on. The features' patterns are then scaled back to
   weigh together what they did (`keep_feature_share`), so a level picks plain gaps, fences and signs as often as
   before and only the mix of features changes. Is the shape right? Should the oldest features drop below 1 (they
   already lose some share to the newer ones)?
2. **"The most picks" against the patterns' own weights.** The curve multiplies each pattern's own weight, so a
   feature with many patterns can still out-pick a new one with a single light pattern (the screech's six patterns
   weigh 5.0 in all, the host's one 0.8, the vent screech's one 0.4). Measured, a level's newest built feature is
   the most picked in City 2 (ceilings), City 3 (pulsing fences), Gangland 1 (screeches) and Marketplace 1 (fence
   generators: its own Barnacle Turret isn't built yet, so its newest built things are Gangland 3's), and nearly
   in Marketplace 2 (vent screeches, 14% of the feature picks against the cyborg's 15%); it is second in Gangland 3
   (generators, behind the screech) and Dead Zone 1 (hosts, behind the screech), and third in Gangland 2 (the
   Octodog, behind the previous level's screeches and ramps). Where a level's own new enemy isn't built yet, its
   newest built feature can be a light one: Marketplace 2's vent screeches get 6–7% of Corporate 1's and 2's
   feature picks, Dead Zone 1's hosts 3–7% of the Golden Zone's. Should the curve set each feature's share of the
   picks directly, whatever its patterns weigh (the placeholder keeps the patterns' weights, which say how rare
   each one is meant to be)?
3. **Features with a cap.** Some rules cap how many of a feature fit (one hover truck early in the campaign, one Bad
   Dream chase at a time, drone waves 20 s apart), so extra picks of a capped feature are dropped and leave their
   stretch empty. Over the campaign (The Hush's remix apart) the curve moves the per-level averages by −1.0 enemies
   in all (of about 220) and −6.2 rows of obstacles (of about 660). The biggest moves: Golden 1 −1.4 enemies and
   −3.0 rows (its hosts, two levels old, are picked 2.5 times a level instead of 0.8, and all but about one are
   dropped), Corporate 2 −0.9 enemies (+1.1 rows), Corporate 1 −0.7 enemies; Gangland 1 +2.1 enemies (−1.8 rows),
   Marketplace 2 +1.1 enemies, Dead Zone 1 +0.9 enemies (−1.6 rows), City 3 +3.5 rows. Fine, or should the curve
   leave a feature alone once its rules' cap is reached (or should a dropped pick's stretch be filled again)?
4. **Older features lean on the guarantee more.** In levels that bring several new things, the oldest feature
   gets fewer picks, and its rules drop some of those: built without the every-feature guarantee, Gangland 3 has
   no cyborg in 10 of 27 layouts (2 before), Gangland 2 in 6 (1), Gangland 1 and City 3 in 4 (1); Dead Zone 1
   no fence generator in 11 (6); Corporate 1 and 2 and Golden 1 no speed pad in 5–6 (2–3). The guarantee still
   places every feature in every layout. Acceptable?
5. **Level weights on top of the curve.** Marketplace 2's vent screeches (2.5×) and Corporate 2's military
   (1.5× drones, hover trucks and Buzz Overdrives) multiply the curve's factor. Keep both, or drop Marketplace 2's
   now that the curve favours its new vent screeches anyway?

**Dead Zone 2, The Hush** (GDD §5: "fewer enemies but more hosts and Bad Dream chases, darker lighting, and long
silent stretches broken by sudden threats")

6. **Quiet stretches and bursts.** Placeholder (`data/levels/dead_zone_2.tres`): 14 s quiet stretches, patterns
   4 s apart, with no enemies but hosts, alternating with 7 s bursts of threats only (holes, fences, signs and
   enemies), 0.9 s apart, the spacing other levels reach only at full difficulty. Per 100 m: 0.03 enemies and 1.0
   row of obstacles in the quiet stretches, 1.3 enemies and 1.9 rows in the bursts (the level used to average 0.8
   enemies and 1.6 rows everywhere). A level: 11.7 enemies besides its hosts (21.2 before) and 33 rows of obstacles
   (41). With fewer picks, more of its features come only from the every-feature guarantee: built without it, a
   layout has no fence generator in 19 of 27 (2 before), no speed pad in 19 (4), no window cyborg in 14 (1) and no
   Octodog in 24 (19); the guarantee still places each one. Right lengths and densities? Bursts could go tighter
   than 0.9 s, which no level has used yet (not playtested).
7. **Threats that outlast a burst.** A hover truck (20–30 s), a drone (until a pad hurls it) and a Bad Dream's
   chase (20–30 s) that start in a burst carry on into the next quiet stretch. An Octodog needs a clear floor for
   its charges, which the dense bursts rarely leave, so its rules find its spot in a quiet stretch about half the
   time (0.5 Octodogs a level there, 0.5 in bursts). Acceptable, or should a quiet stretch wait until they're gone?
8. **More Bad Dream chases.** A chase starts only when the player kills a host on purpose (GDD §9.7), so The Hush
   has more hosts: 1.26 a level (1.19 without the recency curve; The Hush before: 1.0, Dead Zone 1: 1.1–1.2), two
   in about a quarter of its layouts. Every one stands in a quiet stretch, where stomping, clawing or dashing into
   it is easy. Picking hosts more often (3.0 host picks a level, 1.6 before) doesn't add more: the host rules fit
   one chase at a time (31 s plus 7 s before the next host), and none across the first drone wave's arrival (the
   drones own the pads from then on, GDD §9.6), which leaves room for two at most in practice. More chases would
   need shorter chases, fewer or later drones in The Hush, or more reason to kill a host (a bigger bonus). Which,
   if any? Placeholder: hosts picked 5× as often.
9. **Darker lighting.** Placeholder: The Hush's `darkness` 0.7: the scenery gets 51% of its light (the sky, the
   distance fog and the skin's lit surfaces; on screen the floor is about 23% darker, the sky about a third
   darker), never below 30%; glows, the runner and the enemies keep their light. Right amount? The Dead Zone's
   skin (task D5) sets the zone's own palette, and the level's darkness applies on top of it.
10. **Music in the silent stretches** (for task D8, the zone music): should the Dead Zone's music drop away or thin
    out in The Hush's quiet stretches? The generator can list them (`LevelGenerator.quiet_stretches()`).
